Help with funeral costs

If money is a worry, say so at the start. There is usually more help available than people expect, and we will help you get it.

Nobody should be arranging a funeral while frightened of the bill. Please tell us early if cost is a concern — it is an ordinary thing to say, we hear it every week, and it changes what we suggest rather than what we think of you.

Funeral Support Payment

This is the main help available in Scotland. It is paid by Social Security Scotland and, unlike a loan, it does not have to be repaid.

Who can claim

You may be able to claim if you live in Scotland, you are responsible for the funeral, and you or your partner receive one of the qualifying benefits:

  • Universal Credit
  • Income Support
  • Pension Credit
  • Housing Benefit
  • income-based Jobseeker's Allowance
  • income-related Employment and Support Allowance

There are rules about your relationship to the person who died, and about whether another relative might be expected to pay instead. They are not always obvious. Ask us — we deal with this constantly and can usually tell you straight away whether it is worth applying.

How much you get

The payment has more than one part.

  • Burial or cremation costs. Paid in full, including the burial plot or the cremation fee, and certain related charges such as the doctor’s certificate where one applies.
  • A flat-rate amount for everything else. A single fixed sum towards funeral director’s fees, the coffin, flowers, an order of service and similar costs. You choose how to spend it.
  • Transport costs. Help with moving the person who died where the journey is unusually long, and travel costs for one return journey for the person arranging the funeral.

Things worth knowing

  • It will not usually cover the whole cost of a funeral, but it makes a real difference.
  • It can be paid directly to us as the funeral director, so the money never has to pass through your hands.
  • It covers funerals for adults, children and babies, including stillborn babies.
  • Only one person can receive it for any one funeral.
  • You can apply before the funeral takes place, or up to six months afterwards.

How to apply

Online at mygov.scot (opens in a new tab), by telephone to Social Security Scotland on 0800 182 2222, or on paper. You will need the death certificate details and our invoice or estimate.

The application has to be made by you or your partner — as your funeral director, we're not able to apply on your behalf. But bring what you have to our funeral home and we will go through the form with you, and we can give Social Security Scotland our details so the money comes straight to us once you've applied, rather than sitting in your account.

If you couldn't apply within six months of the funeral because of disruption caused by coronavirus, Social Security Scotland will still accept a late application as if it were on time — explain the circumstances when you apply, or call us and we'll help you explain it.

If the person had a funeral plan

Tell us as early as you can, whoever the provider is. A plan changes what is already paid for and reduces what you can claim, so it matters. If you are not sure whether there was one, we can help you find out.

Children's funerals

There is no burial or cremation fee for anyone under 18 in Scotland, including a stillborn baby, and we make no charge of our own for a child's funeral. More about children's funerals.

Other help

  • The person's estate. Funeral costs are usually the first thing paid from an estate, and banks will often release money for a funeral bill before probate is settled. We can invoice the estate directly.
  • Employer and pension schemes. Many pay a death-in-service or funeral benefit. Worth asking, and easily forgotten.
  • Trade unions and armed forces charities. Both frequently help with funeral costs for members and veterans.
  • A simpler funeral. A direct cremation costs considerably less, and a gathering can be held later, in your own time, for very little.

If none of that is enough

Telephone us anyway. We would far rather have the conversation than have a family go without a funeral they can bear, or take on a debt they cannot manage. There is almost always something that can be worked out.
