Someone has died

There is no hurry. Almost nothing has to be done in the next few hours, and we can help with all of it.

Call us whenever you are ready

01368 863636
One of us will answer, at any hour. There is no charge for the call and no obligation to use us afterwards.

If someone has just died, the first thing worth saying is this: you have more time than you think. Unless you have been told otherwise, nothing needs to happen tonight. You do not have to decide anything about the funeral yet. You do not have to have chosen a funeral director to ring us and ask a question.

What has to happen first

Before anything else, a doctor needs to confirm the death and complete a Medical Certificate of Cause of Death. What that involves depends on where the person died.

At home

Telephone the GP practice during surgery hours, or NHS 24 on 111 out of hours. A doctor or nurse will come. If the death was expected, there is no emergency — you do not need to call 999. When you are ready, ring us and we will come to bring the person who has died into our care, at whatever hour suits you.

In hospital

The ward staff will guide you and will contact the hospital bereavement office. For the Royal Infirmary of Edinburgh and the other Lothian hospitals, that office will telephone you to arrange collection of the certificate. The person who has died will stay in the hospital mortuary until we collect them, so there is no rush at all.

In a care home

The home will contact the GP and will usually contact us on your behalf if you have told them who you would like to use. You are welcome to spend as long as you want with the person before they are moved.

Suddenly, or unexpectedly

If a death is sudden, unexplained or accidental, it is reported to the Procurator Fiscal. This is routine and does not mean anything is wrong. The Fiscal may ask for further examination before the death can be registered, which can add a few days. We deal with the Fiscal's office regularly and will keep you informed throughout.

What we do when you call

We will ask a small number of practical questions — where the person is, whether a doctor has been, and whether there is anything we should know. Then we will arrange to bring them into our care. We do not discuss money on that first call unless you want to.

Then, in the following days

  1. Register the death. This must be done within eight days in Scotland. You can do it at any registration office in the country — it does not have to be where the person lived or died. How to register a death.
  2. Meet us to arrange the funeral. At our funeral home, or at your own kitchen table if you would rather. What happens at that meeting.
  3. Tell the organisations that need to know. The registrar will offer you Tell Us Once, which notifies most government departments in a single step.
  4. Check whether help with the cost is available. Funeral Support Payment can be paid straight to us, so the money never has to pass through your hands. Help with funeral costs.

Questions people ask us first

Do I have to decide on a funeral director straight away?

No. And if you have already asked someone to bring the person into their care, you are still free to change your mind afterwards. You are never locked in.

Can I see the person who has died?

Yes, whenever you would like, for as long as you would like. Some families come every day, some come once, some would rather not — all of that is normal. Just tell us and we will make the arrangements.

What does it cost to bring someone into your care?

Nothing, at that point. Our full prices are published on our price list and we will give you a written estimate before you commit to anything.

I am not in Dunbar. Can you still help?

Yes. We look after families across East Lothian and Berwickshire, and we can bring someone home from anywhere in the country or from abroad. More about repatriation.
