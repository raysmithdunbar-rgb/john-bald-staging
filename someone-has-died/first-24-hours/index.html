The first 24 hours

Most of what feels urgent is not. Here is what genuinely needs doing today, and what can wait until you have slept.

If you would rather just talk to someone, telephone us on 01368 863636. Any hour.

Straight away

1. A doctor confirms the death

Nothing else can proceed until this happens. If the person died at home and the death was expected, telephone the GP surgery, or NHS 24 on 111 outside surgery hours. There is no need to dial 999 — an ambulance cannot change anything and the arrival of blue lights makes a hard moment harder.

If the death was sudden or unexpected, do dial 999.

2. Take whatever time you want

There is no rule that says the person who has died must be moved within any particular time. If you want to sit with them, do. If family are travelling and want to see them at home first, that is entirely possible. Tell us what you would like and we will work around it.

3. Telephone us when you are ready

We will come and bring the person into our care. We cover Dunbar, East Linton, Haddington, North Berwick and the whole of East Lothian and Berwickshire, at any hour.

Things that do not need doing today

  • Choosing a coffin
  • Deciding on burial or cremation
  • Setting a date
  • Telling anyone other than close family
  • Cancelling anything, or contacting any bank
  • Deciding anything at all about money

Within the first day or so

Collect the Medical Certificate of Cause of Death

The doctor or the hospital bereavement office will tell you where and when to collect it. In Scotland this certificate is often sent electronically to the registrar, in which case you will not need to handle it at all — the registrar will confirm when you book your appointment.

Find the person's papers, if you can

It helps to know whether there is a will, whether there was a funeral plan, and whether they ever wrote down what they wanted. None of it is essential today, but it saves questions later. If there is a funeral plan with any provider, tell us — it changes what you are entitled to claim.

Book the registrar

You have eight days, and appointments in East Lothian are usually available within two or three. How registering works.

What we will have done by then

By the time you come to see us, the person who has died will be in our care at our funeral home in Dunbar. We will have made no decisions on your behalf and spent none of your money. When we meet, we will go through everything properly, give you a written estimate, and you can take it away and think about it.
