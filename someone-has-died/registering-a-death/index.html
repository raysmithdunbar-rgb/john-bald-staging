Registering a death in East Lothian

You have eight days in Scotland, and you can register at any office in the country. Here is what the appointment actually involves.

The thing most people do not know

In Scotland you may register a death at any registration office in the country. It does not have to be where the person died, or where they lived. If it is easier to register near your own home than to travel back to East Lothian, you can.

Where to go in East Lothian

East Lothian Council's registration service operates from:

John Muir House
Brewery Park, Haddington, EH41 3HA
Telephone: 01620 827308
Email: haddingtonregistrars@eastlothian.gov.uk

Appointments are required — you cannot turn up. Book by telephone or through the council's online booking service. Appointments are usually available within two or three days, so the eight-day limit is rarely a problem.

If you live in the Borders

Families in Berwickshire register with Scottish Borders Council instead. We have listed the offices across the region, with contact details and booking arrangements, in our registrar directory.

How long you have

Eight days from the date of death. That is longer than in England and Wales, where the limit is five. If the death has been reported to the Procurator Fiscal, the clock effectively pauses until the Fiscal releases it — you will not be penalised for a delay that is not yours.

Who can register

Any of the following, in rough order of who is usually asked:

  • A relative of the person who died
  • Someone who was present at the death
  • The executor, or the person's solicitor
  • The occupier of the building where the death happened
  • Anyone else who knows the details and is accepting responsibility for the funeral

What to take with you

The Medical Certificate of Cause of Death is usually sent to the registrar electronically now, so you may not need to carry anything. Take what you can find of the following — the registrar will work with whatever you have:

  • The person's birth certificate
  • Their marriage or civil partnership certificate
  • Their National Insurance number
  • Their NHS medical card, if there is one
  • Any pension or benefit details
  • Your own photo identification

If you cannot find some of it, go anyway. Missing paperwork almost never stops a registration.

What the registrar will ask

The full name and any previous names, date and place of birth, occupation, usual address, marital status, and the names and occupations of the person's parents — including their mother's maiden surname. This last one catches people out, so it is worth asking a relative before you go.

What you come away with

  • The Certificate of Registration of Death (Form 14). This is what we need before the funeral can go ahead. It is free.
  • An abbreviated extract of the death entry. Also free.
  • Full extracts — the "death certificates". These are chargeable, and you will need one for each bank, pension provider, insurer and share registrar. Most families need somewhere between four and eight. Buying them at the appointment is cheaper than ordering them later.

Tell Us Once

The registrar will offer you the Tell Us Once service. It notifies HMRC, the Department for Work and Pensions, the Passport Office, DVLA, and East Lothian Council's own services in a single step, so you do not have to write the same letter a dozen times. It is worth the few extra minutes at the appointment.

It does not cover banks, private pensions, insurers or utility companies — those you still have to contact yourself.

What we do

We cannot register the death for you — that has to be someone connected to the person who died. But we will tell you when the certificate has reached the registrar, help you work out how many extracts to buy, and take care of everything that follows from it. If you are unsure about any of it, telephone us on 01368 863636.
