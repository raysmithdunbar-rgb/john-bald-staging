"Eco funeral" covers everything from a willow coffin at an ordinary crematorium to a shrouded burial in a wildflower meadow. It is worth knowing which choices actually change the impact and which are mostly appearance.

## The choices that make a real difference

**Skipping embalming.** Embalming fluid is formaldehyde-based and enters the ground with the body. It is very rarely necessary — it is required for repatriation by air, and occasionally where a funeral is delayed — and refrigeration handles almost every other case. It is also the eco choice most people have never been told they can make.

**A biodegradable coffin.** Willow, bamboo, pandanus, banana leaf, cardboard or a shroud, with no plastic, metal, screws or staples. See our [coffin range](/coffins/).

**Natural burial rather than conventional.** No embalming, no non-biodegradable materials, no headstone, and the ground managed as meadow or woodland rather than mown grass.

**Natural fibres.** Cotton, wool or linen rather than synthetic clothing.

**Local everything.** A coffin shipped from the other side of the world is not improved by being made of leaves. Ask where it was made.

## Cremation versus burial, environmentally

This is genuinely arguable, which is not what people expect to hear.

**Cremation** uses a substantial amount of energy per cremation and releases emissions, though modern crematoria — Borders Crematorium is among the most efficient in Europe — have reduced this considerably. It uses almost no land.

**Conventional burial** uses land in perpetuity, plus a hardwood coffin, a granite headstone quarried and shipped, and often embalming fluid. Grounds are typically mown and maintained indefinitely.

**Natural burial** avoids nearly all of that, and is the option with an unambiguous answer.

If the environment is your priority, natural burial is the clear choice. Between conventional burial and cremation, decide on other grounds.

## What a natural burial ground is like

Meadow or woodland rather than rows. Graves marked by a tree, a wildflower patch, or nothing at all. Usually no headstones — and for some families that is the sticking point, so it is worth visiting before deciding.

Every site has its own rules about coffins, memorials and what may be placed on a grave. Our [cemetery directory](/directory/cemeteries/) notes what each one allows.

## You can be selective

You do not have to take all of it. A great many families choose a willow or cardboard coffin for an entirely conventional funeral at their local crematorium, and that is a real reduction in impact for no change to anything else.

Equally, if a traditional funeral is what the family needs, no one should feel they have failed a test. Choosing a cardboard coffin because it is the right thing for you is very different from choosing one because you feel judged.

## What it costs

Not necessarily more. Cardboard coffins are the least expensive available by a clear margin. Woven willow and bamboo cost more than a basic veneered coffin but less than solid hardwood. Natural burial ground fees are broadly comparable to council cemetery fees.
