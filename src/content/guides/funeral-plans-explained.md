A pre-paid funeral plan lets you pay for your funeral now, at today's prices. Whether that is a good idea depends on things worth understanding properly first.

## Start with the free bit

You can walk into most independent funeral directors and record what you want for your funeral without paying anything at all. Burial or cremation, which church, hymns, readings, who to tell, what you absolutely do not want.

It is not legally binding. But it removes the guessing, and the guessing is the part that torments families. If you do nothing else, do this — it costs nothing and it helps more than a plan does.

## What a plan guarantees

**The funeral director's services are guaranteed.** Whatever those cost by the time the plan is used, they are covered. This is the real value, and over twenty or thirty years it can be considerable.

**Third-party costs usually are not.** The crematorium fee, the burial fee and the minister's fee are typically covered by an *allowance* rather than a guarantee. If those rise faster than the allowance grows — and crematorium fees have risen quickly — your family may have a shortfall to make up.

**This distinction is the most important thing about any funeral plan, from any provider.** Ask about it directly, and get the answer in writing.

## Regulation

Since **July 2022**, pre-paid funeral plans have been regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority. Before that the sector was largely unregulated, and some providers behaved badly — high-pressure selling, opaque terms, and in a few cases failure.

Regulation changed that materially. Check any provider on the FCA's Financial Services Register before signing. If a provider is not on it, do not proceed.

Your money is held in a trust separate from the funeral director. If the funeral director ceases trading, the provider arranges another from their network.

## Paying

Usually a single payment, twelve monthly instalments interest-free, or a longer period of up to fifteen years with interest. On the longer options, check what happens if you die before completing — some plans pay out, some return premiums, and the difference matters.

## When a plan is a good idea

- You want the certainty that it is dealt with
- You have firm views about your funeral
- You would rather your family did not have to find several thousand pounds
- You expect to stay in the same area

## When it is not

- **You have savings you could set aside instead.** Money in an account remains yours, stays accessible, and can be used for something else if circumstances change. A plan cannot.
- **You might qualify for [Funeral Support Payment](/guides/funeral-support-payment-explained/).** A fully paid plan cuts the flat-rate element from £1,327.75 to £162.05. If your family would likely qualify, a plan can leave them worse off overall. This is not widely explained, and it should be.
- **You may move a long way.** Plans can transfer, but it is not always seamless.
- **You are being pressed.** Nobody should be sold a funeral plan on a doorstep or over the telephone. Walk away.

## Questions to ask before signing

1. Exactly which third-party costs are guaranteed, and which are allowances?
2. What happens if I die before finishing the instalments?
3. What if I move away?
4. What if I cancel — how much do I get back, and when?
5. Is the provider on the FCA register?
6. What commission does the funeral director receive?

A good funeral director will answer all six without hesitation, including the last one.
