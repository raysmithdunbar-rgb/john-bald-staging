Houndwood is the nearest crematorium to Dunbar, about sixteen miles south on the A1 between Cockburnspath and Reston. For families in Dunbar and along the Berwickshire coast it is often both the closest and the most fitting choice.

## What it is like

It is a church, not a purpose-built crematorium, and that is the whole character of the place. Houndwood Church was built in the mid-1830s and held its first service in 1836, when it was known as Renton Chapel. It is B-listed, and it was renovated and became a crematorium in 2015.

Inside there are traditional pews, tall arched windows and a great deal of natural light. Outside there is an Italianate tower and open Berwickshire countryside. Families who find modern crematoria clinical very often prefer it, and families who wanted a church service but have no church connection find it gives them something close to one.

The chapel seats 120, with standing room beyond that for a large funeral — considerably more than most crematoria in this part of Scotland.

## Access — please read this first

The chapel is over 180 years old and stands on a hill. **The crematorium itself advises that the site may not be accessible to people with mobility difficulties.**

This is not a small caveat and we would rather you heard it from us than discovered it on the day. If anyone in your family uses a wheelchair, walks with difficulty, or would struggle with a slope, telephone the crematorium before you commit to a date. They are honest about it and will talk you through what is and is not manageable.

Where access rules Houndwood out, East Lothian Crematorium at Musselburgh is level throughout and about forty minutes from Dunbar.

## Practical detail

There are 30 parking spaces around the church and the site.

The chapel has a music system and screens for photographs and visual tributes, and services can be webcast for family who cannot travel. There is room for singers and musicians if you would like live music rather than recorded.

Houndwood offers attended services, attended direct cremations — where you can be present in the chapel with the coffin and choose the music, but there is no service — and unattended direct cremations.

## The grounds and memorials

The memorial grounds are open every day, at any hour. Memorial options include tree vaults, benches, plaques and a dedication in the Book of Remembrance. Ashes can be scattered in the Gardens of Remembrance, and you are welcome to witness the scattering if you would like to.

The Book of Remembrance can be viewed Monday to Friday, 9am to 5pm, and on the last Saturday of each month from 9am to 3pm.

## Office hours

Monday to Friday, 9am to 5pm, and the last Saturday of each month, 9am to 3pm. The grounds themselves are open 24 hours.

## Getting there from Dunbar

About sixteen miles, roughly twenty-five minutes down the A1. It is a straightforward run and considerably shorter than the journey to any of the Lothian crematoria.
