Burial

In a local churchyard, a council cemetery, or a family lair that has been waiting a long time.

Burial is still what many families in this part of Scotland choose, particularly where there is an existing family grave. We arrange burials throughout East Lothian and Berwickshire and know the local cemeteries and their keepers well.

Choosing where

In East Lothian, burial grounds are managed by East Lothian Council; in Berwickshire, by Scottish Borders Council. Some churchyards are managed by the church itself. We have set out every burial ground in the region, with who manages it and how to enquire about a lair, in our cemetery directory.

An existing family lair

If the family already holds a lair, it can usually be reopened. We will need to trace the lair certificate — the deed showing who holds the burial rights — and get the written permission of whoever holds them. If nobody can find the certificate, do not panic. The council keeps its own records and we can almost always trace it. Allow a little longer for the funeral if we are working from council records.

A grave can be reopened only if there is room and if the depth allows. The council will confirm this before anything is arranged.

A new lair

Where a family has no existing grave, we will help you choose and purchase a new lair. You are buying the exclusive right of burial for a set period, not the land itself. That right can be passed on, and it is what allows the grave to be reopened later.

Worth knowing before you decide

Burial usually costs more than cremation, mostly because of the lair purchase and the interment fee, both of which are council charges rather than ours. If cost is a concern, say so early — it is a completely ordinary thing to weigh, and there is often help available.

What the day looks like

Most families have a service first — in a church, at our funeral home, or at the graveside itself — and then go to the cemetery for the committal. Some have only the graveside service. Both are entirely usual.

We will carry the coffin, or arrange for family to carry if you would like to. A lot of people find that they want to and had not realised it was allowed. It is.

What it costs

Our own fee is on our Standardised Price List. On top of that sit two council charges we pay on your behalf and pass on without markup: the lair purchase, where a new grave is needed, and the interment fee. These vary between East Lothian and Scottish Borders and change each April.

There is no interment fee in Scotland for anyone under 18. More about children's funerals.

Memorials

A headstone cannot go up straight away — the ground needs several months to settle, usually between six and twelve. Cemeteries also have rules about size, material and inscription. We will tell you what is allowed where, and there is no hurry at all. More about memorials.
