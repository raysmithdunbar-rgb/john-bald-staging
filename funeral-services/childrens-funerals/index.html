When a baby or child dies

There is no charge for this. Not from the council, not from the crematorium, and not from us.

We are very sorry that you are reading this page.

There is nothing useful anyone can say about why this has happened. What we can do is take every practical thing off you, tell you plainly what your choices are, and give you as much time as you need to make them. There is no hurry at any stage.

There is no cost

In Scotland there is no burial or cremation fee for a child under 18, or for a stillborn baby. Councils do not charge, and neither do the crematoria we work with. We make no charge either — no professional fee, no coffin charge, no charge for our cars or our time.

Nobody should be making decisions about their child's funeral with one eye on what it costs. If anyone tells you otherwise, please telephone us.

Take your time

You do not need to decide anything today, or this week. Some families arrange things within days because they want it done; others wait a fortnight or more until family can travel or until they simply feel able. Both are right. We will keep your baby or child safely in our care for as long as you need.

Being with them

You can spend as much time with your child as you want, whenever you want, for as long as you want. You can hold them. You can bring brothers and sisters, grandparents, anyone you would like. You can bring a blanket, a toy, a photograph, something you have knitted.

Some parents want to wash and dress their child themselves. You can. Some would find that unbearable, and would rather we did it. That is right too, and we will do it with the same care you would.

Some families take hand and footprints, or a lock of hair. If you would like that and have not thought to ask, we can arrange it — including afterwards, if it occurs to you later.

Stillbirth and very early loss

Everything on this page applies equally to a stillborn baby. The hospital will discuss your options with you, and you are free to use us rather than the hospital's own arrangements if you would prefer — you are entitled to choose, and choosing does not cost you anything.

For a pregnancy loss before 24 weeks, hospitals in Lothian and the Borders offer respectful arrangements including cremation and burial. If you would rather make your own, we will help you do that. Please just ask. Nothing about this is too small a loss to arrange a funeral for.

The funeral itself

Whatever you would like it to be. Some families want a full service in a church. Some want six people at a graveside. Some want a short blessing and nothing else. We have helped with all of these.

Things families often ask about, and the answer is yes to all of them:

  • Carrying your child yourself, rather than us doing it
  • Brothers and sisters being involved — choosing music, drawing on the coffin
  • Colours instead of black, and asking people not to wear dark clothes
  • A small white coffin, or a woven willow one, or one you decorate yourselves
  • Holding the service somewhere that is not a church or a crematorium
  • Having no service at all, and doing something later instead

Burial or cremation

Both are free for a child in Scotland. Councils in East Lothian and the Scottish Borders set aside particular areas for children's burials, often with different rules about memorials that allow more than a conventional grave would — toys, windmills, ornaments. We will show you where they are.

If you choose cremation, ashes from a baby are sometimes very few or none at all, depending on how tiny they were. The crematorium will be honest with you about what to expect, and so will we. It is better to know beforehand.

Financial help

Beyond the fees that are already waived, Funeral Support Payment is available in Scotland for the funeral of a baby or child, including a stillborn baby, if you or your partner receive certain benefits. It can be paid straight to us. More about Funeral Support Payment.

Support, whenever you want it

Not now, necessarily. Perhaps in six months, when everyone else has gone back to normal. These organisations are there either way, and we have listed how to reach them on our bereavement support page — including baby-loss support in Edinburgh and the Lothians, national stillbirth and neonatal charities, and support for bereaved brothers and sisters.

Whenever you are ready, telephone 01368 863636. You will speak to one of us. There is no charge for anything, including asking.
