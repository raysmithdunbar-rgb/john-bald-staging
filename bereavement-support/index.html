Bereavement support

Grief does not run to the funeral's timetable. These are here whenever you want them, including a year from now.

The weeks after a funeral are often harder than the week before it. Everyone else goes back to normal, the cards stop arriving, and you are left with it. That is not a sign anything is wrong with you — it is simply what happens, and it catches almost everybody by surprise.

Our door stays open

A lot of people come back in months later, sometimes years, just for a blether. It is never an imposition and there is never a charge. If you would rather speak to someone who was there at the time than to a stranger, telephone 01368 863636 or call in.

Practical things, afterwards

Grief and paperwork arrive together, which is unfair but unavoidable. A few things that catch people out:

  • Tell Us Once covers government departments but not banks, private pensions, insurers or utilities. Those still need doing individually.
  • Bereavement Support Payment is a separate thing from Funeral Support Payment, and is for people whose husband, wife or civil partner has died. It is not means-tested.
  • Confirmation is the Scottish equivalent of probate. A solicitor is not always needed for a small estate.
  • Anniversaries. The first one is hard, and so is the second, which nobody warns you about.

Locally

NHS Lothian and NHS Borders both run bereavement services attached to their hospitals, and your GP can refer you for counselling. Churches across East Lothian and Berwickshire run bereavement groups that are open to anyone, whether or not you attend. If you would like us to point you at something near Dunbar, ask — we know what is running locally and who is good.
