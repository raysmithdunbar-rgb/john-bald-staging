How much does a funeral cost in Scotland?

What sits in the headline price, what gets added afterwards, and the one question to ask anyone who quotes you a figure.

Funeral costs are confusing for a specific and avoidable reason: the same word covers two quite different things.

Professional fees and third-party fees

The funeral director’s fee covers their own work — collecting and caring for the person who has died, the coffin, the hearse, the arrangements, the paperwork, and staff on the day.

Third-party fees are money the funeral director pays out on your behalf: the crematorium or the council, the minister or celebrant, the newspaper for a notice. These are set by other people and simply passed on.

Some advertised prices include third-party fees and some do not. That is the single biggest reason two quotes can look hundreds of pounds apart when they are actually similar.

So the question to ask is always: does that include the cremation or burial fee?

The Standardised Price List

Since 2021 every funeral director in the UK has been legally required to publish a Standardised Price List, in a prescribed format, within one click of their homepage. It must show an attended funeral and an unattended funeral, with the same things included.

This is genuinely useful and underused. It means you can put two funeral directors’ lists side by side and compare like with like without having to interrogate anybody. Our own is on our Standardised Price List page.

Where the variation actually is

Burial versus cremation. Burial usually costs more, mainly because of the lair purchase and the interment fee, which are council charges. In some areas the gap is substantial.

Attended versus unattended. A direct cremation costs considerably less than an attended funeral, because there is no service, no cars and no staff on the day.

The coffin. The range is wide — recycled cardboard at one end, solid hardwood at the other, with woven willow and bamboo between. See our coffin range.

Everything optional. Cars, flowers, newspaper notices, orders of service and catering add up quickly, and are the first things families reduce when they look at an estimate properly.

What you should always be given

  • A written estimate, itemised, before you commit to anything
  • A clear separation of the funeral director’s own fees from third-party costs
  • The chance to take it away and think about it

If you are not given these, ask. If you are still not given them, go elsewhere.

If the cost is out of reach

  • Funeral Support Payment covers burial or cremation in full plus a flat-rate contribution, and can be paid straight to the funeral director.
  • The estate normally pays the funeral bill, and banks will usually release funds for it before anything else is settled.
  • Employers, pension schemes, trade unions and armed forces charities frequently pay a funeral benefit. Easily forgotten, and worth asking.
  • A simpler funeral is not a lesser one. A direct cremation followed by a gathering of your own, months later, is a perfectly good funeral and costs a fraction.

Children’s funerals

There is no burial or cremation fee anywhere in Scotland for anyone under 18, including a stillborn baby. Many independent funeral directors, ourselves included, make no charge of their own either.
