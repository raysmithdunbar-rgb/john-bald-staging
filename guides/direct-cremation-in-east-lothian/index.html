Direct cremation in East Lothian

What it actually involves, and the questions worth asking before booking one online.

A direct cremation is a cremation with no funeral service and nobody present. The person is collected and cared for, taken to the crematorium at a time the funeral director arranges, and the ashes are returned to the family afterwards.

It is the fastest-growing choice in the UK, and the reasons are usually one of three: the person said plainly they wanted no fuss, the family is scattered and would rather gather properly later, or the cost of a full funeral is out of reach.

All three are good reasons.

Is it dignified?

Yes, and this is the question nearly everyone asks.

The person who has died is cared for exactly as they would be for any other funeral, by the same people, in the same place. The only difference is that there is no service at the crematorium and no mourners in the chapel.

With a local funeral director you can still visit beforehand, as often as you like. Choosing a direct cremation does not mean giving that up.

You can still have a funeral

A direct cremation and a memorial are not alternatives.

Many families have the cremation quietly and hold something of their own weeks or months later — in a village hall, a garden, a pub, a church — with the ashes present if they want them to be. Freed from the crematorium’s clock and the undertaker’s diary, these gatherings are often better than the funeral would have been.

Local versus national providers

Direct cremation is advertised heavily by national online companies. Some are perfectly good. But there are real differences worth knowing before you book.

Where the person is kept. National providers often move people long distances to a central facility. A local funeral director keeps them locally.

Whether you can visit. Usually possible locally; usually not with a national provider.

Which crematorium. National providers typically use whichever is cheapest, which may be a long way from here. Locally, it will be Houndwood or East Lothian Crematorium, both within about half an hour of Dunbar.

How the ashes come back. Locally, you can collect them. Nationally, they are often posted.

Who answers the telephone. A contact centre, or the person actually doing the work.

None of this makes national providers wrong. But the price difference is often smaller than it appears once you compare what is included, and the differences above are worth something.

What it costs

Considerably less than an attended funeral, because there is no service, no cars and no staff on the day.

Always ask whether the price includes the cremation fee. Some headline prices are professional fees only, with several hundred pounds added afterwards. Every UK funeral director must publish a Standardised Price List showing an unattended funeral, which makes this straightforward to check.

Help with the cost

Funeral Support Payment applies to a direct cremation exactly as it does to any other funeral, and can be paid straight to the funeral director.

What happens, step by step

  1. You telephone, and the person is brought into the funeral director’s care.
  2. The paperwork is completed and the cremation registered.
  3. You visit, if you would like to.
  4. They are taken to the crematorium at an arranged time.
  5. The ashes are collected and held until you are ready.
  6. You collect them, or they are buried or scattered for you.
