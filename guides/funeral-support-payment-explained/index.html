Funeral Support Payment explained

Who qualifies, what it pays, and why it can go straight to the funeral director rather than through your account.

Funeral Support Payment is help with funeral costs for people in Scotland on certain benefits. It is paid by Social Security Scotland, and unlike a loan it does not have to be repaid.

Who can claim

You may qualify if all of these apply:

  • You live in Scotland
  • You or your partner receive a qualifying benefit
  • You are responsible for the funeral
  • You apply in time

The qualifying benefits are Universal Credit, Income Support, Pension Credit, Housing Benefit, income-based Jobseeker’s Allowance, and income-related Employment and Support Allowance.

There are also rules about your relationship to the person who died, and about whether a closer relative might be expected to pay instead. These are not obvious, and plenty of people who would qualify assume they would not. Ask.

How much you get

The payment has three parts.

Burial or cremation costs. Paid in full — the burial plot or the cremation fee, and certain related charges.

A flat rate for everything else. For 2026/27 this is £1,327.75 where there was no funeral plan, or £162.05 where the person had a fully paid-up plan. You choose how to spend it: the funeral director’s fee, the coffin, flowers, an order of service.

Transport costs. Help with moving the person who died where the journey is unusually long, plus one return journey for the person arranging the funeral.

The average total award is a little over £2,000.

It will not cover everything

It is not meant to. It is a substantial contribution rather than the whole bill, and most families still have something to find. If what remains is still out of reach, say so to your funeral director — a simpler funeral costs considerably less, and most independents would far rather have that conversation than send a family into debt.

How to apply

Online at mygov.scot, by telephone to Social Security Scotland on 0800 182 2222, or on paper.

You can apply from the date of death up to six months after the funeral. You do not have to wait until the funeral has happened, which matters, because knowing the money is coming changes what you feel able to arrange.

If you couldn’t apply within that six months because of disruption caused by coronavirus, Social Security Scotland will still accept a late application as if it were on time — explain the circumstances when you apply.

The application itself has to come from you or your partner. A funeral director cannot submit it for you, though they can go through it with you and, once you’ve applied, take payment directly instead of you.

Have it paid to the funeral director

This is the part most people do not realise. The payment can go directly to the funeral director rather than to you. The money never passes through your account, and you never have to find it and wait to be reimbursed.

Tell Social Security Scotland when you apply, and tell your funeral director too.

If there was a funeral plan

Say so early, whoever the provider is. A fully paid plan reduces the flat rate from £1,327.75 to £162.05, because most of the cost is already covered. Not declaring it causes problems later.

Children and babies

Funeral Support Payment applies to the funeral of a baby or child, including a stillborn baby. It sits on top of the fact that there is already no burial or cremation fee for anyone under 18 in Scotland.

See our guide on when a baby or child dies.

If you are refused

You can ask for a redetermination, and then appeal. Citizens Advice Scotland will help you free of charge, and they are good at it.

Figures are for the 2026/27 tax year and were checked in August 2026. Rates are uprated each April — check mygov.scot (opens in a new tab) for the current position.
