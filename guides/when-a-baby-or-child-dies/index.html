When a baby or child dies

There is no cost for this in Scotland. And there is no hurry, whatever anyone implies.

We are sorry you are reading this.

This page is deliberately practical, because in our experience that is what is useful at this point. The feelings are not something a web page can help with, and there is a list of people who genuinely can at the end.

There is no cost

In Scotland there is no burial or cremation fee for anyone under 18, including a stillborn baby. Councils do not charge and crematoria do not charge.

Many independent funeral directors — ourselves included — make no charge of their own either: no professional fee, no coffin charge, no charge for cars or time.

If anyone presents you with a bill for a child’s funeral, question it.

There is no hurry

You do not have to decide anything today, or this week. Some parents arrange things within days because having it done helps. Others wait a fortnight or longer, until family can travel or until they simply feel able. Both are entirely normal.

Your funeral director will keep your baby or child safely in their care for as long as you need. Ask, if nobody has told you.

Being with them

You can spend as much time with your child as you want, whenever you want. You can hold them. You can bring brothers and sisters, grandparents, anyone.

You can wash and dress them yourself if you would like to. You can bring a blanket, a toy, a photograph, something you have knitted. If you would rather not do any of that, that is right too, and no one will think anything of it.

Hand and footprints, and a lock of hair can be taken, and many parents are glad afterwards to have them. If it has not been offered, ask — it is a very ordinary request.

Stillbirth and early loss

Everything here applies equally to a stillborn baby. The hospital will discuss its own arrangements with you, and you are entitled to use a funeral director of your own choosing instead. Choosing does not cost you anything.

For a loss before 24 weeks, hospitals in Lothian and the Borders offer respectful arrangements including cremation and burial. If you would rather make your own, you can. Nothing is too early a loss to hold a funeral for, if a funeral is what you want.

The funeral itself

Whatever you want it to be. Things parents often ask about, where the answer is yes:

  • Carrying your child yourself
  • Brothers and sisters choosing music, or drawing on the coffin
  • Asking people not to wear black
  • A small white coffin, a woven willow one, or one you decorate yourselves
  • Holding it somewhere that is not a church or a crematorium
  • Having no service at all, and doing something later instead

Ashes

If you choose cremation, be aware that ashes from a very small baby may be few or none at all. The crematorium should tell you honestly what to expect beforehand. It is better to know than to be surprised.

Financial help

Beyond the waived fees, Funeral Support Payment is available in Scotland for a baby or child’s funeral, including stillbirth, if you receive certain benefits. It can be paid straight to the funeral director.

Support

Not necessarily now. Possibly in six months, when everyone else has moved on and you have not.

  • Held In Our Hearts — Edinburgh-based baby loss counselling and befriending across the Lothians, from people who have been through it themselves. heldinourhearts.org.uk
  • Sands — the stillbirth and neonatal death charity. 0808 164 3332
  • Child Bereavement UK — when a child of any age dies. 0800 02 888 40
  • Cruse Scotland — general bereavement support, including for children. 0808 802 6161
